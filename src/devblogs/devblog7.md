In 2022, I found Python coding language. I always thought coding and scripting was so cool, and
decided it was my time to learn. I studied Python and even began making a silly 
[Medieval RPG game](https://github.com/BoximusMaximus/DND_Game) with Python (It was rough, but
the passion was there). I fell in love with learning this language and wanted more. My friends were
using C++ and I wanted in. I began learning C++ and found it was way too much for me at the time. Next
I moved onto C# and I didn't understand it very well until later. Next was Javascript. Javascript 
felt so good to use and so easy to create with. It came naturally to me, at least until I needed to
learn asynchronous operations. 

I spent countless hours studying, practicing, debugging, and playing
around with these languages making simple scripts and solving problems. The joy of finally solving
a problem or understanding a complicated concept was immeasurable. Then came the rise of AI, and
with it, a great and terrible poison in the developer ecosystem.

## The Beginning of the End

All around were the effects of AI. Art was being generated in the blink of an eye, new
software, and apps were springing to life faster than ever, and new devs were being born by
the hundred-thousands. Everything was moving at light speed and
ideas were being brought to life in a flash. So why is this a problem?

## A Lack of Foundational Knowledge

When I was attending [CodePlatoon](https://www.codeplatoon.org/), there was a student in which 
let AI do all the work for them. Every project was completed by Claude, and every assignment was 
responded to by ChatGPT. On the other hand, there was a student who seldom used AI, and only
for mundane tasks in which they already understood. The difference? Basic foundational knowledge. This
brings me to my final point.

## Why Were Not Getting Another [John Carmack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carmack)

People want a finished product, and AI gives them the option to cut **big** corners. Why do I need to
understand a quaternion when AI can just write the code to handle one? The passion I felt to learn
and gain new knowledge is rarely shared around my peers. The new mindset has become: "Use AI or 
you'll be left behind." 

The more we let machines think and create for us, the more we deny ourselves
the knowledge to architect better technology. Our senior devs will begin to die out as the 
pursuit of knowledge becomes less desirable. Here we sit hoping AI improves while learning nothing
in the meantime.

## The Solution

Understand **what** code you're writing, and **why** it' being written. Using AI isn't inherently 
bad. Even this websites CSS was derived from an AI template, but it all makes sense to me and none
of it jeopardizes my users. Stop cutting corners, start writing your own code, and learn to read
documentation. Using AI when you have no foundational knowledge is dangerous and irresponsible for
customer ready apps. 

I hope you enjoyed my perspective on today's development ecosystem. Goodbye for now.
